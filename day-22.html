Session 1 — Why n8n next to GHL + first login and UI tour

≈ 1.5 hours

GHL workflows are powerful, but they hit a wall: they can't reshape data with real logic, call arbitrary third-party APIs, chain AI models, or round-trip spreadsheets. n8n is a visual automation tool where every step ("node") receives JSON, does something, and passes JSON on. Agencies pay a premium for people who can bridge GHL and n8n — that combination is exactly what this week trains, and it's the differentiator line on your resume.

Session 2 — First real workflow: Schedule → Set → Email

≈ 2.5 hours

The classic first build: every morning at 9:00, n8n emails you "n8n is alive". Trivial output, but it forces you through the three core motions — adding nodes, connecting a credential via OAuth, and testing before activating.

Session 3 — The mental model: items, JSON, expressions, pinning

≈ 1.5 hours

Ten minutes of theory now saves hours of confusion on Days 23–27. Everything in n8n is items flowing between nodes.

Session 4 — Webhook drill: test URL vs production URL

≈ 2 hours

Webhooks are the front door GHL will knock on for the rest of the week. Build a tiny "echo" workflow and burn the test-vs-production URL distinction into memory — it is the #1 cause of "my integration stopped working" tickets.

📦 Deliverable of the day

Two working, activated n8n workflows — Daily Hello (Schedule → Set → Gmail, tested to your inbox) and Webhook Echo (called on both its test and production URLs, verified in the Executions list) — plus both workflow JSONs in GHL Portfolio/n8n-exports, two screenshots, and a journal entry explaining items, expressions, pinning, and test vs production URLs in your own words.

💡 Pro tip

Keyboard habits that make you look senior in a screen-share: Tab on the canvas opens the nodes panel, Ctrl/Cmd+S saves, and double-clicking a node opens its parameters. Also: rename every node the moment you add it — an n8n canvas that reads like a sentence is a portfolio asset in itself.
