Session 1 — Build the "New Patient Inquiry" form

≈ 2.5 hours

Forms are how strangers become contacts. The single most important idea today: every form field should be mapped to a contact field (standard or custom). Mapped fields write into the contact record automatically; unmapped junk fields create data that goes nowhere.

Session 2 — On-submit behavior, submissions & sticky contacts

Session 3 — Build the "Patient Satisfaction Survey" with conditional logic

≈ 2 hours

Surveys are multi-step forms with slides and conditional logic — the answer to one question decides which question comes next. That's the mechanism behind lead-qualification quizzes, one of the most requested builds in agency work.

Session 4 — Embed options + wrap-up

≈ 0.5–1 hour

📦 Deliverable of the day

A live, styled New Patient Inquiry form with every field mapped (Insurance Provider proven writing into the contact record), sticky contact on, thank-you message configured, and 3 test submissions — plus a 5-question Patient Satisfaction Survey whose "Poor" answer conditionally jumps to the improvement question, tested down both branches — with three screenshots and a Day 6 journal entry.

💡 Pro tip

Before building any client form, list the questions in a doc and decide the mapping FIRST: which standard field, which existing custom field, or which NEW custom field each answer needs. Fields you create before building the form appear ready-to-drag in the builder. Form-first builders end up with orphaned, unmapped data — mapping-first builders look like pros.

⚠️ Watch out

A generic "Dropdown" element dragged from the standard elements panel is NOT the same as dragging your Insurance Provider custom field from the Custom Fields group. The generic one stores its answer only in the submission; the custom-field one writes to the contact record. If your mapping test in Session 2 had failed, this mix-up would be the first thing to check.
