Session 1 — The 10 interview questions (with answers to adapt)

≈ 1.5 hours

GHL interviews are refreshingly predictable: a screening call, these questions in some order, then usually a test task. For each pair below: read the sample answer, rewrite it in your own words in your notes, then say it out loud once without reading. Your versions should reference your builds by name.

Session 2 — Test-task gym: the 3 classics under time pressure

≈ 2.5 hours

Most GHL hiring ends with a test task, and it's almost always one of these three. You'll do all three today under real conditions: timer visible, no pausing, done-not-perfect. Use a fresh folder in Ironworks Fitness (or a new test sub-account) so you're not leaning on existing work.

Session 3 — Freelance vs employed, and pricing without guessing

≈ 1 hour

Short and factual. Employed/retainer (agency seat, full-time VA role): steady income, someone else finds the clients, fastest learning because you touch many accounts — but capped rates and you're building their asset. Freelance: higher ceiling and you keep the client relationships — but you spend unpaid hours selling, and income is lumpy for months. The boring, correct answer for most beginners: take the steady seat (or 2–3 anchor clients) first, freelance up from there. Neither path pays anything until applications go out — the quota is the plan.

Session 4 — The 7-day plan, final reflection, and the last checkbox

≈ 1 hour

Day 31 is the dangerous one — no page telling you what to do. So write the next 7 days now, while you're still in program mode. The grind from here is real: expect dozens of applications per interview at the start. The people who get hired are simply the ones still applying in week 3.

⚠️ Reality check, said with love

No program — this one included — can guarantee you income or a job, and anyone who does is selling something. What you have is real and rare: demonstrable skills in a tool thousands of agencies run on, proof on video, and a repeatable practice method. The variable left is volume and persistence: 10 applications a day, honest proposals, and showing up in communities. Silence in week one means nothing. Keep the streak.
