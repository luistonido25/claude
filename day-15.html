Session 1 — Tour the Automation area + workflow anatomy

≈ 1.5 hours

Everything this week happens in one place: the Summit Dental sub-account → left sidebar → Automation. A workflow is always the same shape: one or more Triggers (the event that pulls a contact IN) followed by a vertical chain of Actions (what happens to that contact, step by step, top to bottom). Burn that sentence in — it's the answer to "what is a workflow?" in every interview.

Session 2 — The trigger & action safari

≈ 2 hours

You cannot design automations for a client if you don't know what's on the menu. This session is a disciplined browse of EVERY trigger and action category. You're not memorizing all of them — you're building a mental index so that when a client says "when someone books, do X," you instantly know which trigger fires.

Session 3 — Build, publish & test "Hello World"

≈ 2.5 hours

Time to build your first real workflow end to end — including the part most beginners skip: testing it with a real test contact and reading the Execution Logs. From today on, no workflow of yours is ever "done" until you've watched it run in the logs.

Session 4 — Settings tab deep dive + rebuild from memory

≈ 1.5 hours

The Settings tab inside a workflow is where pros are separated from dabblers. These switches decide whether a contact can be spammed twice, texted at 3 a.m., or messaged after they already replied.

📦 Deliverable of the day

A Training folder in Summit Dental's Automation area containing a published, log-verified "Hello World" workflow (Tag Added → Email → Wait 1 min → Add Tag) plus a from-memory rebuild, a reusable test contact (Testy McTestface), two screenshots (day15-workflow-canvas.png, day15-execution-logs.png), and a journal entry with your top-10 trigger list and Settings-tab notes.

💡 Pro tip

Adopt the professional definition of "done" today and never let it go: a workflow is done when it is Published, has been fired by its real trigger with a test contact, and the Execution Logs show every step green. Canvas-looks-right is not done. You'll repeat this checklist on every build for the rest of the program — and the rest of your career.
