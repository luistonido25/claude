Session 1 — Opportunities vs contacts + build the pipeline

≈ 1.5 hours

Get the mental model right first: a contact is a person; an opportunity is a potential deal attached to that person, sitting at one stage of one pipeline with a dollar value. One contact can have several opportunities (Maria can have a "whitening" deal AND an "Invisalign" deal). Pipelines are the columns those deals move through.

Session 2 — Create opportunities (both paths)

≈ 2 hours

There are two manual ways to create an opportunity — from the board and from a contact record — and automations make them a third way (Week 3). Practice both manual paths until they're boring.

≈ 2 hours

📦 Deliverable of the day

Two working pipelines in Summit Dental — Patient Acquisition (6 stages of flow: 5 stages + won/lost statuses) holding 10 valued opportunities including one Won and one Lost, and Reactivation seeded with 2 cold-list deals — plus board and report screenshots in your portfolio folder and a journal entry that defines all four opportunity statuses.

💡 Pro tip

Steal this stage-naming rule for every pipeline you ever build: each stage is a state, past-tense or noun ("Contacted", "Consult Booked"), and the whole pipeline reads left-to-right as the customer's journey. When a client's pipeline has stages like "Call them!!" you now know exactly what to fix — and saying so in an interview shows senior-level thinking.

⚠️ Watch out

Don't add "Won" and "Lost" as pipeline stages — beginners do this constantly and it double-counts revenue in reports because GHL already tracks Won/Lost as statuses on every opportunity. If you inherit an account with a "Won" column, that's a cleanup item to flag.
