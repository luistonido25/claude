Day 6Portfolio M2: AI Lead Scorer

By tonight, every new row landing in your Day-4 Leads sheet gets read by an AI model, scored 1–10 with a two-sentence summary, written back into the sheet — and if it scores 7+, a 🔥 HOT LEAD alert hits your Telegram. The engineering star of the build isn't the AI call; it's the guardrails around it: a strict-JSON prompt, safe parsing, and an error handler for the day the model rambles.

Session 1 — OpenAI key, connection, and sheet prep

≈ 1 hour

One credential and two sheet columns before any canvas work. Note the double billing reality up front: the OpenAI module costs Make 1 operation per call like any module — but OpenAI bills the tokens separately on your OpenAI account. Two meters, two invoices.

Session 2 — Trigger, filter, and the strict-JSON prompt

≈ 2 hours

Flow: Watch Rows on the Leads sheet → filter out already-scored rows → AI call. The prompt is the contract: you're not asking the model to chat, you're demanding a machine-readable object — and you'll still assume it can break that contract.

Session 3 — Safe parsing, the hot/cold router, write-back

≈ 2 hours

Now the engineering: Parse JSON turns the model's text into real score/summary chips, an error handler catches the day it doesn't, and a router splits hot from standard. Day 3 and Day 2 skills, composed.

Session 4 — Hot lead, cold lead, broken model: prove all three

≈ 1.5–2 hours

Craft the test data to force each path. Feed rows in through the Day-4 webhook if you want the full pipeline demo (intake machine writes the row, scorer picks it up), or paste rows straight into the sheet — both are legitimate; the Loom looks best with the webhook.

⚠️ Two meters, one reputation

AI modules bill twice: 1 Make operation per call AND tokens on the provider account. Before any client work: set a spending cap on the provider account (you did — keep that habit), cap Max Tokens in the module, and keep the not-yet-scored filter in front of the AI call so a re-run can never re-bill an entire sheet. And the sk-… key is a password: never in a screenshot, a Loom frame, or a blueprint you share (connections aren't exported, but check anyway).
